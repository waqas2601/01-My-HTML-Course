1. Introduction

Education is a fundamental pillar of societal development, influencing economic growth, social progress, and political stability. Female education, in particular, plays a crucial role in shaping communities by empowering women, reducing poverty, and fostering national development. In Pakistan, where gender disparities in education remain a significant concern, understanding the role of female education in societal development is essential. Despite policy interventions, barriers such as cultural norms, economic constraints, and inadequate infrastructure continue to hinder women's educational opportunities. This literature review explores the impact of female education on economic growth, social well-being, and political participation while highlighting the challenges faced in Pakistan.

2. Thematic Review of Literature

2.1 Historical Context of Female Education in Pakistan

The history of female education in Pakistan is marked by persistent struggles against socio-cultural restrictions. Initially, access to education was limited to elite women, while rural and lower-income groups faced significant barriers. Government initiatives such as the National Education Policy and Millennium Development Goals have aimed to improve female literacy rates. However, progress remains slow, with UNESCO (2020) reporting a female literacy rate of 49%, one of the lowest in South Asia.

2.2 Economic Impact of Female Education

Studies indicate that female education directly contributes to economic growth by enhancing workforce participation, productivity, and entrepreneurship. Research by Aslam (2018) found that educated women in Pakistan contribute significantly to household income and GDP. Furthermore, educated women are more likely to engage in entrepreneurship, leading to job creation and poverty reduction. The World Bank (2021) highlights that increasing female secondary education by one year can boost Pakistan’s economic growth by 0.3% annually.

2.3 Social and Cultural Benefits

Female education plays a crucial role in improving health outcomes, reducing early marriages, and fostering gender equality. Studies by Khan and Jabeen (2019) indicate that educated mothers in Pakistan are more likely to prioritize their children’s education and healthcare. Additionally, female literacy is linked to lower infant mortality rates and improved maternal health. UNICEF (2021) reports that areas with higher female literacy rates witness reduced child marriage cases, emphasizing the transformative power of education.

2.4 Political and Legal Influence

Educated women are more likely to engage in political and social activism, advocating for gender rights and legal reforms. Pakistan has seen an increase in female political participation, with more women running for office and engaging in policy-making. Government programs such as the Benazir Income Support Program (BISP) have facilitated greater educational access for women, but implementation challenges persist (Rahman, 2021). Research suggests that greater female representation in governance leads to policies that prioritize education, healthcare, and social welfare.

2.5 Barriers to Female Education in Pakistan

Despite the evident benefits, several obstacles hinder female education in Pakistan:

Cultural Norms: Many families prioritize male education over female education due to traditional beliefs.

Economic Constraints: Poverty forces many girls to drop out of school to contribute to household income.

Early Marriages: UNICEF (2021) highlights that child marriage remains a key factor limiting educational attainment.

Lack of Infrastructure: Rural areas lack proper educational facilities, qualified teachers, and transportation services.

3. Gaps in Existing Research

While several studies explore the role of female education in development, research gaps remain:

Regional Disparities: There is limited research on how female education varies between urban and rural areas in Pakistan.

Impact of Technology: The role of digital learning and online education for women in Pakistan remains underexplored.

Long-Term Policy Effectiveness: There is a need for further studies on the long-term impact of government policies promoting female education.

4. Conclusion

This literature review underscores the significant role of female education in Pakistan’s economic, social, and political development. While there has been progress, major challenges persist, requiring policy reforms, infrastructure development, and awareness campaigns. Addressing these issues will be crucial for achieving gender equality and fostering sustainable societal development in Pakistan.

The current state of female education in Nagar is the result of a complex process involving various local and global actors, such as local activists, NGOs, and conservative religious leaders. The development of education in Nagar has also been influenced by rapid changes in the region, including political reforms and the construction of the Karakoram Highway in the 1970s. Over the past one or two decades, Nagar has developed a solid education system for girls, especially at the primary and middle school levels. Today, most people in Nagar highly value female education, and many parents are willing to invest in their daughters’ education, including higher education. This shift in mindset is particularly remarkable in a traditionally conservative and male-dominated society, where such a change would have been unthinkable just a few decades ago.

This significant change has had a noticeable impact on local views regarding the roles of women in society. However, opinions are still mixed on the importance of education for girls. Some older people and parents still see education mainly as a way for girls to become better housewives and mothers, while younger women and girls are more focused on education as a path to a career. For young women to secure a good job in Pakistan, the level and quality of education they receive is crucial, but opportunities in mountain areas are still quite limited.

Additionally, changes are happening in family planning as well. Many young women now wish to complete their education and work for a few years before getting married and starting a family. This desire is supported by many mothers who want their daughters to have more control over their own lives. Similar trends have been observed in other rural areas, like Nepal, where families’ support is key to the educational success of girls.

Moreover, female education has allowed more girls and young women in Nagar to travel, not only for better schooling and higher education but also for work opportunities. The idea of women living away from their families is becoming more accepted, which marks an important shift in the traditionally patriarchal society. This growing acceptance of women living independently is a key factor in achieving more gender equality, especially in mountain regions where educational opportunities are limited. Many women migrate to cities for better education and job prospects.

As a result of education and new career opportunities, many women’s self-confidence has significantly improved. This change has influenced local society, as women are now more active in community life and contribute more to household decisions. Even women with little formal education are becoming more involved in public events and starting their own small businesses. Studies have shown that women’s confidence grows when they interact with others, gain literacy skills, and, in some cases, earn their own income.

However, it’s important to note that female education is not the only factor driving these changes. Other factors, such as power dynamics within households and structural inequalities in communities or at the government level, also play significant roles. Gender discrimination remains a major obstacle to achieving educational goals, such as those set by the United Nations. Despite these challenges, studies have shown that educated parents are more likely to send their children to school. With the rapid expansion of both male and female education in Pakistan over the last two decades, it’s likely that the new generation of educated parents will continue to push for progress, just as seen in Nagar.

This study shows that promoting female education is a crucial part of building a more just society, with strong, confident women, in Nagar and in other regions facing similar challenges.

Women’s education in Pakistan is confronted by a range of significant challenges that hinder progress in this vital area. Cultural norms, deeply rooted in traditional gender roles, often limit the access of girls and women to education. In many parts of the country, families prioritize the education of boys over girls, and societal expectations often place more value on women’s roles in the home rather than on their intellectual development. Additionally, poverty remains a major barrier, as many families struggle to afford education-related expenses, such as uniforms, books, and transportation, leading to high dropout rates among girls. Furthermore, the lack of educational facilities in rural and underdeveloped areas exacerbates the problem, with many girls having limited or no access to schools, especially in remote regions.

Despite these formidable obstacles, there are also promising opportunities that can help improve the state of women’s education in Pakistan. One key opportunity lies in the efforts of the government, which has launched various initiatives aimed at increasing access to education for girls and women. These initiatives include building new schools, providing scholarships, and offering financial incentives for families to send their daughters to school. Another vital opportunity comes from the growing movement for women’s empowerment, which is encouraging women to seek education and challenging the traditional norms that have kept them out of the classroom. Increasingly, women in Pakistan are becoming more vocal about their rights to education, and this shift is helping to break down barriers and change societal perceptions.

In addition to these efforts, technology plays a crucial role in expanding access to education for women in Pakistan. Online learning platforms, digital classrooms, and mobile education apps offer women, especially those in rural areas, the chance to learn without having to travel long distances. These technological solutions can also bridge the gap where physical educational infrastructure is lacking, allowing women to access quality learning resources from the comfort of their homes. The growing role of technology in education represents a powerful tool to overcome some of the logistical and financial barriers that women face.

To truly improve women’s education in Pakistan, it is essential to address the challenges head-on while capitalizing on these opportunities. Tackling cultural norms, reducing poverty, and improving the availability of educational facilities must go hand in hand with supporting government initiatives, empowering women, and harnessing the power of technology. By doing so, we can ensure that women in Pakistan have the opportunity to receive an education, unlock their potential, and contribute meaningfully to society.

The cultural norms and traditions in Pakistan have created a situation where men and women are expected to fulfill different roles in the family, with women bearing the majority of household responsibilities. This leads to feelings of anxiety, stress, and both mental and physical exhaustion. As women take on more domestic tasks, they have little free time, which distances them from the outside world and creates barriers to their access to education and employment. In Pakistan's male-dominated society, men hold the power to make decisions on behalf of women, whether they are fathers, uncles, brothers, or husbands.

Research has shown that family structure directly affects the level of restrictions imposed on women. In joint families, parents tend to impose stricter limitations on their daughters compared to nuclear families. Decisions about job choices and marriage are often influenced by the type of family system. In joint families, authoritative and dictatorial decision-making styles are commonly used by parents and guardians when it comes to women's careers. Women are typically not allowed to make decisions about their personal affairs independently. The study also highlights that, after marriage, decision-making about family planning and other matters often shifts to the husband, with the wife expected to follow his directives.

This research further reveals that in male-dominated rural areas, husbands adopt coercive and authoritarian attitudes towards their wives, particularly in joint family settings, to assert their dominance. In many cases, husbands prevent their wives from working, and when they do work, they are often harshly treated upon returning home. It is concluded that women will not be able to access equal job opportunities or empowerment unless traditional values are changed. To address this, it is essential to educate and raise awareness, particularly among men, about women's rights. Educating males from a young age, both formally and informally, is crucial in ensuring gender equality. Public awareness about women’s rights should be included in educational curricula from primary school to university level.

Additionally, both government and non-government organizations should organize programs, seminars, conferences, and lectures to promote gender equality. Religious leaders, especially scholars, can play an important role by sharing examples from Islamic history, where men and women were granted equal rights in areas such as health, education, and politics, thereby promoting the importance of equality in society.
